# Demo 5: use the browser

**What it shows:** Claude can open websites in the built-in browser, read
them, click through them, and hand the tab back to the user.

The demo site is the EPLUS model viewer (BIM model review):
`https://models-eplus.ashyflower-c2e54274.centralus.azurecontainerapps.io/`
No sign-in. It opens on a 3D data hall model.

What is on the page (checked 2026-09-24):

- **Model picker**: the model name at the top left opens a list of four:
  HAC Design Large Datahall, HAC Design Small Datahall Displaced, HAC Design
  Small Datahall, large-datahall-high-density-tray. Each has its own address.
- Under the name: **Elements** (count) and **Extent** (size in metres), e.g.
  Small Datahall 1,333 elements, 35.1 x 16.8 x 5.7 m; Large Datahall 1,638
  elements, 37.5 x 59.4 x 7.1 m.
- Bottom toolbar: **Fit** (key F; views 1 top, 2 front, 3 right, 4 back,
  5 left, 0 start), **Section** (key X; cut along X, Y or Z with a Bottom/Top
  slider and Reset), **Background** (key B), **Full screen**.
- The 3D scene is drawn on a canvas, so read the page text for names and
  numbers and use screenshots to see the model.

## Steps

1. Say: "I'll open one of our 3D models in the browser and walk through it with
   you. You can take over the tab at any time."
2. Open the site in the browser pane. Read the model name, element count and
   extent, and tell them in one line.
3. Open the model picker, switch to **HAC Design Large Datahall**, and compare
   the two in one line (elements and size).
4. Show two controls: press `1` for the top view, then turn on **Section**
   (X) and cut along one axis. Take one screenshot so they see what you did.
   Reset the section afterwards.
5. Tell them the tab is theirs: they can rotate, zoom and click themselves, and
   you can read any website the same way, fill in forms, and pull
   information out of pages.
6. Add the card to the cheat sheet: title "Websites", what "I can open a site
   in the browser, read it, click through it and pull out what you need.",
   try "Open ... and find ..." / "Compare these two pages."

## Close

- What you just saw: "I opened a website, read it, switched models and used
  its tools, and the tab is yours."
- Try saying: "Open <site> and tell me ..." / "Look up ... on the
  manufacturer's site."
